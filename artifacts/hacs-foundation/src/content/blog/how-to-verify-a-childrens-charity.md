## Summary

Donating to a children's charity should feel like a safe, trustworthy act. Unfortunately, not every organisation claiming to help children is legitimate, well-run, or effective. This guide gives you the tools to verify any charity before you donate, so you can give with confidence.

## Step 1 — Check for Formal Registration

A legitimate charitable organisation should be registered with the appropriate government body in the country where it operates. In Nigeria, charities and non-governmental organisations are registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). In the United Kingdom, charities register with the Charity Commission. In the United States, they register with the IRS.

Registration alone does not guarantee effectiveness, but it is a basic sign of legitimacy. Look for the organisation's registration number and verify it directly with the relevant authority if possible.

## Step 2 — Look at Their Website and Contact Information

A credible children's charity should have:

- A working website with a clear description of their programmes
- A physical address and verifiable contact details (phone number, email)
- Staff or leadership who can be contacted and are accountable
- Information about how donations are used

Be cautious of organisations with no physical address, no verifiable contact details, or websites that contain only emotional appeals without substantive programme information.

## Step 3 — Read Their Child Protection Policy

Any organisation working with children should have a formal child safeguarding or child protection policy. This policy should explain how the organisation protects children from harm, abuse, and exploitation. It should cover:

- How staff and volunteers are screened
- What behaviour is expected around children
- How concerns or complaints can be reported
- How children's privacy and dignity are protected

If an organisation cannot produce a child protection policy, or does not appear to have one, that is a serious warning sign. Read about [child safeguarding at HACS Foundation](/child-safeguarding) to understand what responsible child protection looks like.

## Step 4 — Understand How Donations Are Used

A transparent charity should be able to explain clearly how donated funds are used. Look for:

- An annual report or financial report (even a simple one)
- A breakdown of how much goes to programmes versus administration
- Information about specific activities funded by donations

Be cautious of organisations that promise exact outcomes ("your £10 feeds a child for a month") without explanation, or those that claim 100% of donations go to children without any discussion of operational costs.

## Step 5 — Check Their Online Presence and Reviews

Search for the organisation's name and look at:

- News coverage (local or national media)
- Community reviews or feedback from people who have visited or engaged with them
- Social media activity (authentic engagement, not just fundraising posts)
- Whether any concerns have been raised publicly

Absence of negative coverage is not the same as a positive track record, but repeated, credible concerns from multiple independent sources should prompt caution.

## Step 6 — Ask Direct Questions

It is entirely reasonable to contact a charity directly before donating. A trustworthy organisation will welcome your questions. Ask:

- How long have you been operating?
- How many children do you currently serve?
- What specific programmes does my donation support?
- Can I see your registration documents?
- Do you have a child protection policy I can review?
- How do you report on your impact?

Pay attention to how they respond — not just what they say. An organisation that is evasive, defensive, or unable to answer basic questions may not be the right choice.

## Warning Signs to Watch For

Some patterns suggest an organisation may not be what it claims:

- **No formal registration** or unwillingness to provide a registration number
- **Unverified photographs of individual children** used extensively in fundraising, often without consent language
- **Vague or unverifiable impact claims** ("we've helped thousands of children")
- **Pressure tactics** that create urgency and discourage questions
- **No physical address** or contact details that don't work
- **Recently created organisations** with no track record, especially around holidays or news events
- **Inconsistent information** about leadership, location, or programmes

## Give With Confidence

Taking a few minutes to verify a charity before donating is not a sign of cynicism — it is a sign of care. When your donation reaches a verified, effective organisation, it achieves its intended purpose. When it does not, it can cause harm without your knowing.
