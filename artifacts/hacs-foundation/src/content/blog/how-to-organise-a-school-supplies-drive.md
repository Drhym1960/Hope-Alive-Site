## Summary

A school supplies donation drive is a practical, tangible way to support children's access to education. When organised well, a drive can collect exactly the items children need, build community spirit, and connect donors directly to a worthwhile cause. This guide walks you through every step — from initial planning to final delivery.

## Step 1 — Choose Your Partner Organisation First

Before you collect a single item, contact the organisation you want to support and ask what they actually need. This is the most important step in any donation drive.

Organisations often receive donations of items they already have in abundance, while genuinely needed supplies go uncollected. By asking first, you ensure your drive collects useful items rather than creating a storage problem.

[Contact HACS Foundation](/contact) to ask what school supplies are most needed for children in our education programme. We can provide a specific list based on the current academic year and the needs of children in our care.

## Step 2 — Set a Clear Goal and Timeframe

A drive with a clear, specific goal generates more engagement than a vague, open-ended appeal. Consider:

- **A specific item goal**: "We are collecting 100 exercise books and 50 geometry sets"
- **A value goal**: "We aim to raise ₦50,000 in school materials"
- **A timeframe**: Two to three weeks is generally long enough to build momentum without losing focus

Share the goal publicly so that donors can see progress and feel part of a collective effort.

## Step 3 — Identify Your Collection Point

You need a visible, accessible place where donors can drop off items. Good options include:

- A reception desk or common area in a workplace
- A classroom or staff room in a school
- The foyer of a church, community hall, or sports club
- A local business that is willing to host a drop-off box

Label the collection point clearly with the name of the drive, what items are accepted, the deadline, and the name of the charity that will receive the donations. Consider using a decorated box or bin to make it visible and inviting.

## Step 4 — Create a Simple Needs List

Put together a clear, specific list of the items you are collecting. A good school supplies list might include:

- Exercise books (specify size and ruling if possible)
- Pencils, pens, and erasers
- Rulers and geometry sets
- Sharpeners
- Colouring pencils or crayons (for younger children)
- Backpacks or school bags
- Water bottles

Keep the list focused. A long list with too many options can feel overwhelming. A short, specific list is easier for donors to act on quickly.

## Step 5 — Promote the Drive

Let people know about the drive through every available channel:

- **Email** to colleagues, classmates, or community members
- **Social media posts** with a clear call to action and donation deadline
- **Posters or flyers** at the collection point and in high-traffic areas
- **Announcements** at meetings, assemblies, or services
- **Personal asks** — the most effective form of fundraising is still one person asking another directly

In your promotion, tell the story of why this matters. Explain that the supplies will go to children who cannot afford them, and name the organisation receiving them. Specific, real information is more motivating than general appeals.

## Step 6 — Track and Report Progress

During the drive, keep donors informed of progress. Update the total regularly on social media or by email. If you have a target, show how close you are to reaching it. Progress updates remind lapsed donors to contribute and encourage others to give before the deadline.

When the drive ends, share a summary: how many items were collected, the total value, and details of the delivery. A photograph of the collected supplies (without identifiable children) makes a compelling close to the campaign.

## Step 7 — Arrange Delivery to the Organisation

Coordinate with the charity to arrange delivery of the collected items. Confirm:

- The delivery address and opening hours
- Any packaging or labelling requirements
- Whether a receipt or acknowledgement will be provided
- Any import or customs considerations if the drive is international

Make the delivery a visible event if possible — it provides a natural end point for the story you have been telling your donors.

## Tips for a Successful Drive

- **Keep it simple** — The easier it is to participate, the more people will.
- **Make it specific** — Donors respond better to "50 exercise books needed" than "school supplies needed."
- **Follow up** — Thank everyone who contributed, publicly and personally where possible.
- **Be transparent** — Share details of the delivery and any feedback from the receiving organisation.
- **Repeat it** — A successful drive becomes an annual tradition. Communities build momentum around regular giving activities.
