## Summary

Giving to charity is a meaningful act. To make sure your donation achieves what you intend, it is worth taking a few minutes to ask some basic questions before you give. This article provides a clear list of questions every donor should consider — and explains what good answers look like.

## Is the Organisation Formally Registered?

Any charity operating in a country should be registered with the relevant regulatory body. In Nigeria, this is the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). In the UK, it is the Charity Commission. In the US, registered charities have IRS exemption status.

Registration does not guarantee effectiveness, but it does mean the organisation is legally accountable. Ask for the registration number and, if you are uncertain, verify it directly with the relevant authority.

## What Specific Programmes Does My Donation Support?

A credible charity should be able to explain exactly what your donation funds. The description should be specific — not just "helping children" but "funding school fees, uniforms, and materials for children in our education programme."

Ask whether your donation is restricted (designated for a specific programme) or unrestricted (allocated where most needed). Both are valid approaches, but it is worth understanding which applies to your gift.

## How Are Donations Used?

Ask for a breakdown of how donated funds are used — specifically, what proportion reaches the people the charity serves versus what is used for administration, fundraising, and overhead.

A very low overhead figure (such as "100% goes directly to the children") is not always a good sign. Organisations need to pay staff, maintain facilities, and manage their operations effectively. What matters most is whether the money is used effectively, not whether overhead is zero.

## Can I See an Annual Report or Financial Summary?

Established charities should be able to share some form of reporting on their activities and finances, even if it is informal. This might be an annual report, a programme update, a list of activities, or a simple financial summary.

If an organisation cannot or will not provide any documentation of their activities, that is a significant concern.

## Does the Organisation Have a Child Protection Policy?

Any charity working with children should have a formal child safeguarding or child protection policy. This document should explain how the organisation keeps children safe from harm, abuse, and exploitation.

Specifically, it should address:

- How staff and volunteers are screened
- Expected standards of behaviour around children
- How children's privacy and dignity are respected
- How concerns can be reported and addressed

If an organisation does not have a child protection policy — or cannot produce one when asked — this is a serious red flag. Read about [child safeguarding at HACS Foundation](/child-safeguarding) for an example of what responsible practice looks like.

## How Will I Know My Donation Made a Difference?

Ask whether the organisation provides updates to donors about the impact of their contributions. This might be a newsletter, an annual report, a thank-you message that describes specific outcomes, or access to programme updates.

You do not need a detailed audit, but some form of communication that connects your donation to real outcomes is a reasonable expectation from any credible charity.

## Is the Organisation Transparent About Challenges?

Honest organisations acknowledge difficulties. Fundraising does not always go as planned. Projects encounter obstacles. Staff change. A charity that presents an unbroken record of success and never mentions any challenges or setbacks may be more invested in its public image than in honest communication.

Good charities communicate honestly about what is working, what is not, and how they are addressing difficulties. This kind of transparency is a positive sign.

## Are Children's Images and Stories Used Responsibly?

Be attentive to how an organisation uses photographs and stories of children in its communications. Responsible organisations:

- Obtain informed consent before sharing photographs of children
- Do not use identifiable images of children without consent
- Tell stories in ways that respect children's dignity, rather than using poverty or suffering as a marketing tool
- Do not share private information about individual children's circumstances

If you see an organisation sharing graphic, undignified, or clearly non-consensual images of children, consider that a warning sign.

## Can I Contact Someone Directly?

A functioning organisation should have a working phone number, email address, and physical location. You should be able to contact a real person and receive a response. This is a basic indicator of whether an organisation actually exists as described.

## What Happens If I Need to Request a Refund?

Ask about the charity's donation refund policy. Most charitable donations are non-refundable, but reputable organisations should have a clear, fair process for handling exceptional cases. Read more about [transparent donation policies at HACS Foundation](/transparency).

## A Final Word

Asking questions before you donate is not a sign of distrust — it is a sign of care. Credible organisations welcome scrutiny because they have nothing to hide. An organisation that is evasive, defensive, or unable to answer basic questions about its work is sending you a message.
