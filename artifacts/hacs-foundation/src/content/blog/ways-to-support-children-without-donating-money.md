## Summary

Money matters enormously to children's charities — but it is not the only currency of support. Time, skills, goods, networks, and advocacy are all valuable. If you want to help vulnerable children but cannot make a financial donation right now, there are still many meaningful ways to contribute.

## Volunteer Your Time

Volunteering is one of the most direct ways to contribute to a children's organisation without a financial donation. Volunteers can help with:

- **Administrative support** — filing, data entry, managing records, or responding to correspondence
- **Communications** — writing, editing, photography, social media, or graphic design
- **Event support** — helping organise or run fundraising events, community days, or awareness campaigns
- **Skills training** — if you have a professional skill (computing, cooking, tailoring, healthcare), you may be able to share it with older youth in vocational programmes
- **Tutoring and mentorship** — supporting children's academic development, where an organisation has appropriate safeguarding in place

It is important to note that volunteering directly with children requires proper safeguarding checks and organisation-specific training. Never attempt to work directly with vulnerable children without going through a verified organisation's onboarding process.

[Learn more about volunteering with HACS Foundation](/volunteer) and the kinds of support we are looking for.

## Donate Goods and Supplies

Many children's foundations need physical goods that they cannot easily afford to buy:

- School supplies (exercise books, pens, pencils, rulers, geometry sets)
- Clothing and shoes (particularly school uniforms)
- Basic hygiene products
- Food items (non-perishable goods for feeding programmes)
- Office equipment (computers, printers, furniture)

Before collecting or donating goods, always contact the organisation first to confirm what is needed. Unsolicited or irrelevant donations create logistical problems and may not help the children you intend to support.

To support HACS Foundation with goods, [contact us](/contact) to discuss what would be most useful. You can also read our guide on [how to organise a school supplies drive](/blog/how-to-organise-a-school-supplies-drive).

## Share Your Professional Skills

Professional expertise is often in short supply at smaller charitable organisations. If you have skills in any of the following areas, they could be genuinely valuable:

- **Legal advice** — governance, contracts, employment law, or regulatory compliance
- **Accounting and financial management** — bookkeeping, audit support, financial planning
- **IT and technology** — website maintenance, software setup, cybersecurity
- **Healthcare** — medical professionals, nurses, or public health specialists may be able to contribute to health programmes
- **Communications and fundraising** — grant writing, donor communications, annual reports

Skills-based volunteering tends to be flexible and can often be done remotely, making it accessible to people who cannot commit to regular in-person attendance.

## Raise Awareness

Talking about the needs of vulnerable children is a form of support that costs nothing. You can:

- **Share information** about verified organisations on social media, being mindful of child privacy (do not share images of individual children without consent)
- **Speak at your workplace or school** about the importance of supporting children's charities
- **Write for a community publication** or blog about the issues affecting vulnerable children
- **Recommend verified organisations** to friends, family, or colleagues who are looking to give

Awareness raising leads to donations, volunteers, and partnerships that organisations need to grow and sustain their work. Do not underestimate the value of a personal recommendation.

## Advocate for Policy Change

Child poverty, inadequate child welfare systems, and barriers to education are not inevitable — they are the result of policy choices that can be changed. If you want to address the root causes of child vulnerability, advocacy is one of the most powerful tools available.

This might mean:

- Engaging with elected officials about policies affecting children
- Supporting organisations that advocate for child welfare reform
- Participating in awareness campaigns and public consultations
- Voting for policies that prioritise children's wellbeing

## Organise a Community Event

Community events — whether a school fundraiser, a workplace awareness day, a neighbourhood collection, or a community celebration — raise money and awareness simultaneously. You do not need a large budget to organise an effective community event.

Think about what your community does well and how that could be connected to the cause. A bake sale, a sports day, a quiz night, or a concert can all generate meaningful support for a children's foundation.

## Corporate and Organisational Engagement

If you work for a company or organisation, you may be in a position to facilitate a partnership beyond your individual contribution. Many organisations have corporate social responsibility (CSR) programmes that include charitable partnerships. Raising the possibility of a partnership with a verified children's charity is a contribution in itself.

Read more about [how companies can partner with children's charities](/blog/how-companies-can-support-childrens-charities).

## Simply Stay Informed

Staying informed about the needs of vulnerable children — the scale of the problem, the approaches that work, and the organisations doing good work — positions you to act effectively when the opportunity arises. It also makes you a more credible advocate and a better donor when you are in a position to give.
