## Summary

Companies of every size can make a meaningful difference in the lives of vulnerable children — and a well-structured partnership benefits both the business and the children it supports. This article explains the different ways companies can engage with children's charities, how to choose the right partner, and what responsible corporate engagement looks like.

## Why Corporate Partnerships Matter

Children's charities often operate with limited and unpredictable funding. Corporate partnerships provide more than money — they bring in reliable, sustained support, professional skills, and wider community networks. For companies, the benefits include alignment with social values, employee engagement, and positive community impact.

The best corporate partnerships are built on a genuine shared commitment to outcomes for children, not just a transaction between a donor and a recipient.

## Forms of Corporate Support

### Financial Donations and Sponsorships

The most direct form of corporate support is a financial contribution — either a one-off donation, an annual sponsorship, or ongoing programme funding. Companies can choose to give unrestricted funds (which the charity allocates where most needed) or to sponsor a specific programme.

Programme sponsorships, where a company funds a defined activity such as an education programme or feeding programme for a defined period, can be a good fit for companies that want visibility and accountability over how their contribution is used. [Contact HACS Foundation](/contact) to discuss sponsorship opportunities.

### Payroll Giving and Matched Giving

Payroll giving schemes allow employees to donate directly from their salary, often tax-efficiently. Matched giving — where the company matches employee donations pound for pound or naira for naira — doubles the impact of individual contributions and demonstrates organisational commitment.

These schemes are low-cost to administer and can become a valued part of the company's employee benefits and engagement offering.

### Skills-Based Volunteering

Many companies have employees with professional skills that charities genuinely need: financial management, communications, IT, legal advice, project management, and more. Skills-based volunteering, where employees donate time and expertise rather than money, can be more valuable than a financial donation in some contexts.

This form of volunteering also tends to be more satisfying for employees, because the contribution is clearly linked to their professional identity. [Learn more about volunteering with HACS Foundation](/volunteer).

### In-Kind Donations

Companies can donate goods and services that organisations need but struggle to fund: office equipment, vehicles, printing services, catering for events, or professional photography. As with all donations, in-kind gifts should be offered in response to actual needs, not disposed of opportunistically.

Contact [HACS Foundation](/contact) to ask what in-kind support would be most valuable before making arrangements.

### Cause-Related Marketing

Some companies choose to tie a product, campaign, or event to a charity — donating a percentage of sales or event proceeds. This can raise both funds and awareness. It requires clear communication to consumers about what proportion of their purchase benefits the charity and how that money is used. Transparency here is essential.

### Staff Fundraising Events

Sponsored runs, bake sales, sports days, and other staff-led fundraising events are a classic form of corporate-charity engagement. They build team spirit, raise money, and increase employee awareness of social issues. Many charities can provide materials, presentations, and support for companies wanting to run their own events.

## How to Choose the Right Charity Partner

Not every children's charity is the same. Before entering a partnership, a company should do its own due diligence:

- **Is the charity formally registered?** Check that it holds the appropriate registration in its operating country.
- **Does it have a child protection policy?** Any organisation working with children should be able to produce this.
- **Can it account for how donations are used?** Ask for programme reports or financial summaries.
- **Does its values align with yours?** A partnership is more sustainable when both parties share a genuine commitment to the same outcomes.

Read our guide on [how to verify a children's charity before donating](/blog/how-to-verify-a-childrens-charity) for a checklist you can adapt for corporate due diligence.

## Partnership Principles

Corporate partnerships with children's charities work best when they are:

- **Long-term** — Sustained relationships deliver more than one-off gifts. Children's wellbeing is not a short-term project.
- **Transparent** — Both parties should be clear about expectations, contributions, and how impact will be reported.
- **Child-centred** — The wellbeing of children comes before marketing considerations. Avoid approaches that exploit children's images or stories for commercial benefit.
- **Mutually respectful** — The company brings resources; the charity brings expertise. Neither should dominate.
