## Summary

Wanting to help orphaned and vulnerable children is a compassionate impulse. But good intentions matter less than good action. This guide explains how to support children effectively, safely, and in ways that genuinely serve their best interests — not just our own desire to feel helpful.

## Understanding Who We Mean by Vulnerable Children

The term "orphaned and vulnerable children" covers a broad range of situations. Some children have lost both parents. Others have a surviving parent who is unable to care for them due to illness, poverty, or displacement. Still others are children living on the streets, in unsafe home environments, or without access to education or healthcare.

Understanding this range matters because different children need different kinds of support. A child who needs school fees does not have the same needs as a child who needs medical care or a safe place to sleep. Effective help begins with understanding the specific context.

## Start by Supporting Established, Verified Organisations

The most reliable way to help is to give to organisations that have demonstrated a track record of serving children responsibly. Look for foundations that:

- Are formally registered with the relevant government authority
- Publish information about their programmes and how funds are used
- Have a clear child protection and safeguarding policy
- Can be contacted directly and respond to reasonable questions
- Do not share identifiable images or stories of children without consent

[Hope Alive Children Spring Foundation](/about) is a registered charitable foundation in Nigeria that provides shelter, education, healthcare, and psychosocial support to orphaned and vulnerable children in Makurdi, Benue State. Our work is guided by clear safeguarding standards and a commitment to each child's dignity.

## Donate Where Your Money Has the Most Impact

Financial support is often the most practical way to help. When donating, consider:

**Unrestricted gifts** allow the organisation to direct funds where they are most urgently needed. This is often the most valuable kind of donation, because needs change and organisations need flexibility.

**Programme-specific gifts** support a particular area — such as education, feeding, or healthcare — if you have a preference for how your donation is used.

**Recurring donations**, even small ones, are more valuable than single large gifts because they allow organisations to plan ahead and maintain consistent care for children.

## Volunteer Thoughtfully

Volunteering with children's organisations requires careful consideration. Not every act of volunteering helps, and some can cause harm — particularly when it involves short-term contact with children who have already experienced loss and instability.

Before volunteering, ask the organisation:

- What safeguarding checks are required?
- Will I be working directly with children, or in a support role?
- What is the minimum commitment period, and why?
- What training is provided?

Responsible organisations have clear answers to these questions. If they don't, treat that as a warning sign. Read more about [volunteering with HACS Foundation](/volunteer) to understand how we approach volunteer engagement.

## Donate Goods and Supplies Thoughtfully

School supplies, clothing, hygiene products, and food are often needed by children's foundations. Before sending physical goods, always check with the organisation first. Unsolicited donations can create storage problems and may not match the actual needs of the children.

Organised school supplies drives — where donors collect specific requested items — can be very effective. Learn [how to organise a school supplies drive](/blog/how-to-organise-a-school-supplies-drive) if you want to run a collection in your school, workplace, or community.

## Advocate and Raise Awareness

Talking about the needs of vulnerable children — in your community, at your workplace, or through social media — helps build the awareness that leads to action. You do not need to share children's stories or photographs to advocate effectively. Sharing verified information about the issues, the scale of need, and credible organisations is enough.

## What to Avoid

Some well-meaning actions can cause real harm:

- **Sharing identifiable photos of children** without their consent, or the consent of their guardian, violates their privacy and dignity.
- **Visiting orphanages or children's homes** as a tourist activity (sometimes called "voluntourism") can create unhealthy attachments and be harmful to children's development.
- **Donating through unverified crowdfunding campaigns** for individual children can expose you to fraud and may not benefit the children at all. Learn [how to verify a children's charity](/blog/how-to-verify-a-childrens-charity) before giving.
- **Promises you cannot keep** — such as pen-pal relationships or ongoing sponsorship — if you are not in a position to sustain them.

## The Role of Community

Orphaned and vulnerable children do best when they are supported within their communities, not separated from them. The most effective long-term interventions strengthen families and communities so that children are cared for closer to home. When you support a foundation that takes this approach, your donation does more than provide immediate relief — it helps build the systems that protect children for years to come.

At HACS Foundation, our work in Makurdi, Benue State, Nigeria is rooted in community. We work alongside families, local leaders, and partner organisations because we know that no foundation can replace a community that cares for its children.
