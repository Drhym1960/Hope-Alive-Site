## Summary

Street children and at-risk youth are among the most visible yet most misunderstood populations in many cities and towns. Genuinely supporting them requires moving past assumptions, understanding the complexity of their situations, and choosing approaches that respect their dignity and serve their long-term wellbeing.

## Who Are Street Children?

The term "street children" refers broadly to young people who spend significant time on the streets — either because they live there, because they work there to support themselves or their families, or because the street has become their primary social environment. The causes are varied and often interconnected:

- Loss of one or both parents
- Family poverty or unemployment
- Domestic violence or abuse at home
- Displacement due to conflict or natural disaster
- Rejection or abandonment

It is important not to assume that every child on the street is an orphan, or that they are entirely without family. Many maintain some family connection. Effective support recognises this complexity.

## What Genuinely Helps

### Working Through Established Organisations

The most reliable way to support street children is through organisations that have established relationships with the communities and young people they serve. These organisations understand the local context, have trained staff, and operate within child protection frameworks.

[Hope Alive Children Spring Foundation](/about) works with vulnerable children in Makurdi, Benue State, Nigeria — including children who are at risk of street life or who have experienced homelessness. Our programmes provide shelter, education, healthcare, and skills training. [Learn more about our programmes](/programs).

### Providing Education Access

One of the most powerful interventions for at-risk youth is access to education. When a young person has a school to attend, a routine is established, relationships are formed, and opportunities begin to open. Supporting school fees, supplies, and uniforms for at-risk children can prevent many from ending up on the street in the first place.

Read more about [why education support matters](/blog/why-education-support-matters) and how it can change a child's trajectory.

### Skills and Livelihood Training

For older youth who are beyond school age, vocational training and skills development can provide the foundation for economic independence. Training in trades, computing, catering, or other practical skills — combined with business education — can enable young people to support themselves without resorting to unsafe or exploitative work.

### Outreach and Safe Spaces

Effective organisations often operate outreach programmes that go to where children and young people are, rather than expecting them to come in. Safe spaces — drop-in centres, shelters, or community spaces — provide somewhere young people can access food, first aid, and human connection without being required to leave the street permanently as a precondition.

### Health Services and Mental Health Support

Street children and at-risk youth face significant health challenges, including malnutrition, untreated illness, and trauma. Access to basic healthcare and, where available, mental health support is an important component of any comprehensive programme.

## What Does Not Help

### Giving Money Directly on the Street

Giving cash directly to children on the street, however well-intentioned, can place them at risk. Children seen to have money may become targets for exploitation or violence. It may also increase dependence on street activity as a source of income, discouraging engagement with support services.

If you want to help a child you see on the street, the most responsible action is to contact or direct them to a verified local support organisation.

### Photographing Children Without Consent

Taking photographs of street children — particularly without consent — violates their dignity and privacy. These photographs, once shared online, can be used in ways that exploit or misrepresent the children depicted. Responsible organisations have strict policies against the unsolicited photography of children. Read about [child safeguarding and photography consent](/child-safeguarding).

### Short-Term "Rescue" Approaches

Approaches that focus on removing children from the street without addressing the underlying causes are rarely sustainable. A child who returns to the same circumstances after a short period in a shelter has not been helped in any lasting way. Effective support takes a longer view, addressing root causes and working with families and communities wherever possible.

## How You Can Help From a Distance

If you are not in a position to work directly with street children, you can still make a meaningful difference:

- **Donate** to a verified organisation working with at-risk youth. [Support HACS Foundation](/donate).
- **Raise awareness** in your community about the needs of street children and the organisations addressing them.
- **Volunteer your skills** — organisations need help with administration, fundraising, communications, and other non-direct roles. [Learn about volunteering](/volunteer).
- **Advocate for policy** that supports child protection, poverty reduction, and access to education.

## Supporting the Broader Community

Street children do not exist in isolation from their communities. Poverty, family breakdown, and lack of opportunity affect entire communities. Supporting local economic development, access to education, and family strengthening initiatives helps prevent children from ending up on the streets in the first place.
